# F5 Hiring Solutions LinkedIn Video — Edit Design

## Objective

Turn the 86-second, single-take Steve recording into a fast, premium LinkedIn
explainer without losing any examples or core lessons. The finished draft will
feel deliberately art-directed rather than like graphics placed over a webcam
recording.

The one message to retain is: F5 Hiring Solutions provides affordable,
pre-screened, skills-tested, fully dedicated employees without recruiting,
office, or termination fees.

## Source and Output

- Source: `C:\Users\User\Downloads\linkedin-video.mp4`
- Source characteristics: 86 seconds, 1138×640 landscape, one continuous static
  shot, flat white background, Steve right of center, baked F5 logo at top left,
  no b-roll or camera movement.
- Primary output: 1080×1350 (4:5) H.264 MP4 for the LinkedIn feed.
- Preserve the source unchanged. All cuts, isolation, reframing, graphics, and
  audio work happen in derived assets and the HyperFrames composition.

## Editorial Structure

The first pass removes only dead air. It keeps short natural breaths at phrase
and sentence boundaries so the delivery remains human and avoids audio cliffs.
The second pass detects stutters, repeats, false starts, and retakes. Every
candidate is presented with its timestamp, surrounding words, recommendation,
and expected duration saved. No spoken-mistake cut is applied until it is
approved.

All examples and claims in the supplied transcript stay unless the user
explicitly approves their removal. Motion timings are generated from the final
word-level transcript after the cut is locked, with visual entrances beginning
roughly 0.15–0.25 seconds before their exact spoken anchors.

## Visual System

The composition uses a near-black graph-paper world with restrained grid lines,
cool blue light, off-white type, and occasional F5-red emphasis. Liquid-glass
cards are reserved for explanation scaffolds and concise labels; major process,
pricing, and no-fee claims become short full-frame graphic takeovers. There are
no decorative shapes that compete with the message and no full-screen
cross-fades.

Steve is isolated from the white background using a clean luma/color key with a
refined edge matte. He is placed right of center in the 4:5 frame, leaving the
left and lower-left zones for word-synced callouts. The layout may temporarily
center him when a gesture or card collision requires it. A continuous,
seek-safe 3–5% scale drift provides subtle camera movement; section-boundary
repositions are hidden beneath opaque graphic takeovers so there are no visible
zoom resets.

The baked source logo is excluded with the background treatment. A clean F5
Hiring Solutions logo asset will be supplied or reconstructed from an official
brand asset—not traced from memory—and used as a small animated corner bug and
closing mark. The bug remains subordinate to the message and may clear during
full-screen takeovers.

## Transcript-Synced Beat Plan

1. **Identity / hook** — Steve introduction, clean name-and-company glass card,
   then establish the dark graph-paper world.
2. **Dedicated employees** — one employee card enters and locks into a dedicated
   role slot.
3. **Pre-screened candidate** — candidate profile receives a screening check.
4. **Interview** — a simple candidate-to-client interview connection animates.
5. **Skills test** — job-specific/general task card travels through a quality
   gate and receives a verified result.
6. **How it works** — New York company node connects to two India office nodes;
   the employee remains the single travelling subject.
7. **Payment relationship** — `You → F5 → Employee` is drawn only as Steve says
   each part.
8. **Dedicated communication** — direct client/employee line becomes primary;
   F5 recedes.
9. **Friday report** — a weekly report glass card fills with completed-work rows.
10. **Pricing** — `$350 / week` receives a short full-screen liquid-glass/stat
    takeover.
11. **Flat rate / no fees** — recruiting, office, and termination fees resolve
    to zero in sequence, precisely on the spoken list.
12. **Call to action** — the process closes into one complete hiring pipeline,
    followed by a polished `Book a call` card and logo close.

Steve’s original audio continues uninterrupted beneath all full-screen graphic
beats. The speaker remains visible for approximately 40–50% of the running time,
with longer absences used only for process and payoff moments.

## Captions and Typography

Burned-in captions use the final word-level transcript, at most two short lines,
with phrase-based timing and limited keyword emphasis. They stay within LinkedIn
safe areas, never cover Steve’s face, and visually defer to major cards rather
than duplicating them. Typography remains serious and compact; motion emphasizes
meaningful nouns, numbers, and transitions instead of animating every word.

## Audio Treatment

The voice receives conservative broadband denoise, high-pass cleanup, light
compression, de-essing only if needed, and true-peak-safe normalization to
approximately -14 LUFS integrated for social playback. Edits use short
equal-power audio transitions and real room tone rather than digital silence.
Approved subtle interface and transition sound design supports the glass and
process beats. No music bed is added to this draft.

## Review and Quality Gates

The reviewed draft must pass:

- source-to-retimed transcript consistency and anchored beat-sync validation;
- HyperFrames preflight, lint, and composition checks;
- matte inspection around hair, hands, and clothing against the dark background;
- face framing and caption legibility at 960×540 review scale;
- contiguous-frame inspection through every takeover, reframe, zoom, and seam;
- checks for black frames, stale mounts, overlapping cards, and dead visual gaps;
- audio listening at every cut boundary plus loudness and true-peak measurement;
- final assembled-render review, not only isolated scene previews.

The draft delivery includes the MP4, proposed and approved edit decisions,
retimed transcript, beat sheet, composition source, and a `VERIFY.md` report of
checks and any remaining limitations.

## Explicit Constraints

- Keep all examples and core lessons.
- Do not apply mistake cuts before review.
- Do not leave the original flat-white shot as the visual background.
- Do not crop the baked logo awkwardly or allow it to clash with new graphics.
- Do not pause or mute narration during graphic takeovers.
- Do not use arbitrary beat spacing; sync to the actual spoken-word timestamps.
- Do not overwrite or modify the original recording.
